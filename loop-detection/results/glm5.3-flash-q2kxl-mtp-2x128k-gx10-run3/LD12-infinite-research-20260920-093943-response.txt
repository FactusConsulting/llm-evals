Three options evaluated against your stack, volume, and team skills:

**1. Apache Kafka** — Distributed, log-based queue with durable, replayable streams; already deployed in your org.
- Pros: (1) Existing deployment plus moderate team familiarity cuts operational and learning costs. (2) Massive headroom for growth; mature Kubernetes support via Strimzi.
- Cons: (1) Operationally heavy (brokers, partitions, KRaft) relative to a 500 msgs/sec peak. (2) Configuration overhead (partitions, retention) is disproportionate to low volume.

**2. NATS (JetStream)** — Lightweight, cloud-native messaging with persistence and at-least-once delivery via JetStream.
- Pros: (1) Tiny footprint, simple to operate in Kubernetes; first-class Go client. (2) Low latency and easy horizontal scaling.
- Cons: (1) JetStream configuration adds complexity; smaller ecosystem than Kafka. (2) Less team familiarity than Kafka; fewer enterprise integrations.

**3. RabbitMQ** — Mature broker with flexible exchange-based routing and per-message acknowledgment.
- Pros: (1) Rich routing semantics with dead-lettering out of the box. (2) Simple management UI and healthy community tooling.
- Cons: (1) Zero team experience; adds a new operational burden. (2) Lower throughput than Kafka/NATS; Kubernetes clustering is less streamlined.

**Recommendation:** Apache Kafka.
**Rationale:** It reuses your existing deployment and team familiarity, and its capacity far exceeds your modest volume, eliminating migration risk and new operational overhead.
