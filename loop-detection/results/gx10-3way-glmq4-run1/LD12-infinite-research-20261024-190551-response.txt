**Option 1: Apache Kafka**
Distributed, partitioned commit log designed for high-throughput event streaming.
- Pros: Team has moderate familiarity and the org already runs Kafka, so operational knowledge exists; strong durability, replay, and ecosystem.
- Cons: Operationally heavy for this volume — 500 msgs/sec peak is far below where Kafka earns its complexity; running a second cluster (or sharing the analytics one) couples microservices to analytics infrastructure.

**Option 2: NATS JetStream**
Lightweight, Go-native messaging system with persistent streams and simple clustering.
- Pros: Minimal Kubernetes footprint and simple operations; excellent official Go client, and the team already has some exposure.
- Cons: Less team depth than Kafka; smaller third-party ecosystem for analytics/tooling integration.

**Option 3: RabbitMQ**
Mature AMQP broker focused on flexible routing and work-queue semantics.
- Pros: Rich routing topologies and a polished management UI.
- Cons: Zero team experience; clustering and K8s-native operation are more cumbersome, and its strengths (complex routing) aren't needed here.

**Recommendation: NATS JetStream.**
Rationale: At ~50k msgs/day with 500 msgs/sec peaks, JetStream provides the required durability and throughput with the lightest operational burden and best Go ergonomics, while the existing Kafka cluster remains dedicated to analytics rather than being burdened with service messaging.
