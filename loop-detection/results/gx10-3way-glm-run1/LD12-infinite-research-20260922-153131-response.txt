# Message Queue Recommendation

## Option 1: Apache Kafka
Distributed, partitioned, durable log designed for high-throughput streaming.

**Pros:**
1. Already deployed in the org — shared ops knowledge, tooling, and monitoring.
2. Massive headroom for 500 msgs/sec peak; mature Go client libraries.

**Cons:**
1. Operationally heavy (brokers, partitions, KRaft) for only 50k msgs/day.
2. Higher per-message latency than lightweight alternatives; overkill at this volume.

## Option 2: RabbitMQ
Mature AMQP broker with flexible routing via exchanges and per-message acknowledgments.

**Pros:**
1. Rich routing semantics and per-message acks suit task-queue patterns.
2. Simple to run in Kubernetes via the community Operator.

**Cons:**
1. Zero team experience — new learning curve and operational burden.
2. Throughput and horizontal scaling weaker than Kafka/NATS; clustering is non-trivial.

## Option 3: NATS (with JetStream)
Lightweight, Go-native messaging system; JetStream adds persistence and at-least-once delivery.

**Pros:**
1. Tiny footprint, very fast, first-class Kubernetes and Go support.
2. Simple operations — a single binary with easy clustering.

**Cons:**
1. Team has only "some exposure" — JetStream retention/replay semantics need learning.
2. Smaller ecosystem and fewer enterprise integrations than Kafka.

## Recommendation: **Kafka**

**Rationale:** At 500 msgs/sec peak with an existing Kafka deployment and moderate team familiarity, reusing proven infrastructure beats introducing a new system for modest volume.
