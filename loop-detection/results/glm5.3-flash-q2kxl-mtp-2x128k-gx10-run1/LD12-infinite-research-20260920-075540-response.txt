**Option 1: Apache Kafka**
Distributed commit log built for high-throughput, durable event streaming.
Pros: (1) Already deployed in the org — shared tooling and operational knowledge. (2) Strong durability, replay, and ecosystem support.
Cons: (1) Overkill for 500 msgs/sec peak; heavy operational footprint (KRaft, partition tuning). (2) Consumer complexity (offsets, rebalancing) is high for simple service-to-service messaging.

**Option 2: NATS JetStream**
Lightweight, Go-native messaging system with persistent streams via JetStream.
Pros: (1) Simple to run on Kubernetes; small resource footprint, fast to learn. (2) Go-first client libraries; team already has some exposure.
Cons: (1) Smaller ecosystem and fewer managed offerings than Kafka or RabbitMQ. (2) Stream/replica configuration is newer territory for the team.

**Option 3: RabbitMQ**
Mature AMQP broker focused on flexible routing and per-message delivery semantics.
Pros: (1) Rich routing (exchanges, dead-lettering) out of the box. (2) Broad documentation and a strong management UI.
Cons: (1) Zero team experience — training and operational ramp-up required. (2) Clustering and failover on Kubernetes are more finicky than NATS.

**Recommendation:** NATS (JetStream).

**Rationale:** At ~50k msgs/day with 500 msgs/sec peaks, NATS JetStream is right-sized, Kubernetes- and Go-friendly, and leverages existing team exposure — while the org's Kafka remains available if analytics-grade durability demands grow.
