**Message Queue Recommendation**

**1. Apache Kafka**
A distributed, partitioned commit log built for high-throughput event streaming.
- Pros:
  - The org already runs a deployment, and the team has moderate familiarity.
  - Durable replay and strong ecosystem tooling.
- Cons:
  - Heavy operational footprint that is oversized for 500 msgs/sec.
  - Sharing the analytics cluster couples failure domains, while a separate cluster adds cost.

**2. NATS JetStream**
A lightweight, Go-native messaging system with built-in persistence and streaming.
- Pros:
  - A single small binary with an official Helm chart, so it is simple to run on Kubernetes 1.29.
  - A first-class Go client, and it supports both request-reply and pub/sub, which suit microservice patterns.
- Cons:
  - Its ecosystem and connectors are smaller than Kafka's.
  - The team has only limited exposure, so some learning is needed for stream and consumer configuration.

**3. RabbitMQ**
A mature AMQP broker with flexible routing via exchanges and queues.
- Pros:
  - Rich routing, dead-lettering and per-message acknowledgment semantics.
  - The RabbitMQ Cluster Operator simplifies Kubernetes deployment.
- Cons:
  - The team has no experience with it, so ramp-up risk is highest.
  - Quorum-queue tuning and Erlang-level troubleshooting add operational complexity.

**Recommendation: NATS JetStream**

Rationale: At about 50k msgs/day, JetStream provides durable, Go-native messaging with far lower operational overhead than Kafka, and it keeps service traffic isolated from the analytics cluster. Existing Kafka can still receive events via a bridge where analytics needs them.
