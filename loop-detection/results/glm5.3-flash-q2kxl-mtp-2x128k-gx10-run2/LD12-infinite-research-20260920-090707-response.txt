**Candidates evaluated against your stack** (Go services, Kubernetes 1.29, ~50k msgs/day, 500 msgs/sec peak):

**1. Apache Kafka** — Distributed, partitioned, durable commit log built for high-throughput streaming.
- Pros: (1) Enormous throughput headroom with horizontal scaling via partitions. (2) Already deployed in the org, so shared tooling and operational knowledge exist.
- Cons: (1) Heavy operational footprint (broker tuning, rebalances, KRaft management) that is overkill at 500 msgs/sec. (2) More complex consumer model (offsets, partition assignment) and higher latency for simple service-to-service messaging.

**2. RabbitMQ** — Mature AMQP broker with flexible routing and per-message acknowledgments.
- Pros: (1) Rich routing semantics (exchanges, dead-lettering) and per-message acks for workflow-style messaging. (2) Well-supported Kubernetes operator and strong management UI.
- Cons: (1) Weaker throughput and scaling than Kafka or NATS; clustering doesn't shard queues. (2) Zero team experience, adding a new operational learning curve.

**3. NATS JetStream** — Lightweight, cloud-native messaging system with persistent, at-least-once streams.
- Pros: (1) Tiny resource footprint, simple clustering, first-class Kubernetes fit. (2) First-party Go client (nats.go) matches your stack; low latency at your volumes.
- Cons: (1) Smaller ecosystem and community than Kafka or RabbitMQ. (2) Fewer very-large-scale production references; some advanced features less mature.

**Recommendation:** NATS JetStream.

**Rationale:** At 50k msgs/day with 500 msgs/sec peaks, NATS JetStream delivers more than enough durability and throughput with the simplest Kubernetes operations and the best Go integration, while the existing Kafka cluster remains available if analytics-grade streaming needs arise later.
